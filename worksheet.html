Congress voted yes. Why did the money still not arrive?

Congress could only ask the states for money. There was no president to collect it and no court to punish a state that ignored the request, so states voted yes and then sent nothing.

New York taxed goods heading to its neighbours. What could Congress actually do about it?

Almost nothing. Congress had no power over trade, so all it could do was politely ask New York to stop. Even passing the resolution changed nothing.

Armed men were marching on an arsenal. Why couldn't Congress just send an army?

Congress had no army of its own and could not draft a single soldier. It could only ask the states for men and money. In real life a private army paid for by Boston merchants stopped the rebellion.

2. What the Articles actually got right it was not all failure

Word bank — use each once won the war · new states · slavery · king · themselves

a. The Articles held the country together long enough that we won the war and made peace with Britain.

b. The Northwest Ordinance let western land become new states, equal to the original thirteen rather than ruled by them.

c. That same law banned slavery in the whole Northwest Territory.

d. There was no king, and states kept the right to govern themselves.

3. The two numbers that matter

Congress actually received
That is what percent?

In real life Congress asked for about $15 million between 1781 and 1786 and received about $2.5 million — roughly one dollar in six.

4. Think about it

a. Your state probably scored well by refusing to pay. So why did the country end up worse off?

Every state doing the smart thing for itself added up to a country that could not pay its debts, defend itself, or settle arguments. What is good for one state is not automatically good for all thirteen.

b. If you could change one rule of this government, which one — and what problem would it fix?
